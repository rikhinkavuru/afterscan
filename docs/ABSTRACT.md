# Afterscan — abstract (Devpost, ≤ 300 words)

A CT ordered for kidney stones finds a 7 mm lung nodule. The radiologist writes "follow-up CT in 6–12 months recommended." The urologist treats the stone. Nobody owns the nodule. In a 2020 safety-net cohort, 39% of patients with a recommended incidental lung-nodule follow-up had no documented surveillance, and only 28% completed it on time. Some of those nodules were early cancers. Meanwhile, since the 21st Century Cures Act, patients read the signed report in their portal before any doctor explains it.

Afterscan is a web tool for that moment. A patient pastes their radiology report. The tool explains it in plain English, extracts the incidental findings, and converts the published follow-up guideline for each one into dated steps: a calendar file with reminders, a printable plan, a browser-side checklist, and the questions to ask their doctor.

The design separates reading from deciding. A language model reads the report into a strict schema and must quote the exact sentence for each finding; the server verifies every quote against the text and flags anything it cannot find. The plan itself never comes from the model. A deterministic engine encodes six guidelines transcribed from the primary papers (Fleischner 2017 for lung nodules; the ACR Incidental Findings Committee white papers for adrenal, kidney, liver and pancreas; ACR thyroid and TI-RADS), with every branch unit-tested and shown on each card. The engine refuses when a guideline does not apply (screening scans, known cancer, age under 35) and asks for what is missing when the report lacks a size.

We evaluated on 123 synthetic reports with known ground truth, 121 style-paraphrased variants, and 70 real de-identified chest X-ray reports from NIH Open-i, measuring finding recall and precision, quote verification, false alarms on normal reports, and whether an extraction error would have changed the plan. Afterscan stores nothing server-side. It is not medical advice; it is the date and the question list, so the follow-up happens.
